How your redundancy payout is actually taxed

A termination payout isn't taxed as one lump sum — each component is treated differently. Here's how the pieces fit together.

Two different tax treatments in one payout

Your total payout is rarely a single tax event. The genuine redundancy component is tax-free up to a limit, with anything above that limit taxed as an Employment Termination Payment (ETP). Unused annual leave is taxed under its own leave payout rules. Payment in lieu of notice is generally taxed as an ETP as well, not as tax-free redundancy pay. Three components, three sets of rules — which is why a payslip breakdown for a redundancy payout usually looks more complicated than a normal pay run.

The tax-free limit on genuine redundancy pay

For a genuine redundancy specifically, part of the payment is tax-free. The tax-free amount is calculated using a base dollar figure plus an additional amount for each completed year of service, and both figures are set and indexed by the ATO each financial year — so the exact dollar limit changes annually. Anything paid above that tax-free limit is taxed as an ETP: at concessional rates up to a separate ETP cap, then at your marginal tax rate above that cap.

What makes it "genuine" for tax purposes

The ATO's genuine redundancy tax treatment has its own conditions, similar to but not identical to the Fair Work Act's genuine redundancy test. Broadly, the payment needs to result from your job genuinely no longer existing, you need to be under pension age at the time of dismissal, and there needs to be no arrangement to re-employ you. If these conditions aren't met, the whole payment is generally taxed as an ordinary ETP without the benefit of the tax-free component — which is one reason it's worth understanding whether your redundancy meets the genuine test in the first place (see our genuine redundancy guide).

What isn't part of the tax-free redundancy component

Unused annual leave and any long service leave payout are not part of the genuine redundancy tax-free amount — they're taxed under separate leave payout rules, which can mean your marginal rate or a capped rate depending on when the leave was accrued. Payment in lieu of notice is also taxed separately as an ETP rather than folded into the tax-free redundancy figure. This is a common source of confusion when people try to estimate their after-tax total from the gross figure alone.

Where to check exact figures

Because the tax-free limit and ETP cap are both indexed every financial year, this guide deliberately doesn't quote a specific dollar figure — it would be out of date by the time you read it next year. Check the ATO's current genuine redundancy and early retirement scheme payments page for this financial year's exact limits, or have an accountant or tax professional confirm the number against your actual payout.

Disclaimer: this guide explains the general tax treatment mechanism and is not tax or financial advice. Tax-free limits, ETP caps, and eligibility conditions change and depend on your individual circumstances — confirm your exact position with the ATO or a registered tax agent.
