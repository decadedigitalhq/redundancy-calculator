How redundancy pay is calculated

Under the National Employment Standards (NES) in the Fair Work Act 2009, most employees with at least one year of continuous service are entitled to a minimum redundancy payment when their role is genuinely made redundant. The payment is calculated as:

Redundancy pay = base weekly pay × number of weeks (set by years of service)

Less than 1 yearNil
1 year to less than 2 years4 weeks
2 years to less than 3 years6 weeks
3 years to less than 4 years7 weeks
4 years to less than 5 years8 weeks
5 years to less than 6 years10 weeks
6 years to less than 7 years11 weeks
7 years to less than 8 years13 weeks
8 years to less than 9 years14 weeks
9 years to less than 10 years16 weeks
10 years or more12 weeks*

*Redundancy pay reduces to 12 weeks at 10+ years of service, following the Australian Industrial Relations Commission's 2004 Redundancy Case. Small business employers (fewer than 15 employees) are generally exempt from NES redundancy pay entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Does this apply to me if I'm a casual employee?

No. Casual employees are generally not covered by the NES redundancy pay provisions, regardless of how long they've worked for the employer.

What counts as "base weekly pay"?

Your ordinary rate of pay for your normal hours of work. It excludes overtime, bonuses, loadings, allowances, and superannuation contributions.

Is redundancy pay taxed?

A genuine redundancy payment can be partly tax-free up to a limit set by the ATO each financial year, with amounts above that taxed as an employment termination payment (ETP). This calculator estimates your NES entitlement only — speak to a tax professional or check the ATO website for how tax applies to your specific payout.

What if my employer has fewer than 15 employees?

Most small business employers (fewer than 15 employees) are exempt from paying NES redundancy pay. Check your award or employment contract, as some provide redundancy pay even where the NES doesn't require it.

Can my award or contract give me more than this?

Yes. This calculator shows the NES minimum only. Many modern awards, enterprise agreements, and individual contracts provide higher redundancy entitlements — always check what applies to your role.
