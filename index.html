How your payout is calculated

A termination payout can include up to three separate NES entitlements, calculated independently and added together:

Total payout = redundancy pay + notice pay (if paid in lieu) + unused annual leave

*Reduces to 12 weeks at 10+ years, following the 2004 Redundancy Case. Small business employers (fewer than 15 employees) are generally exempt from redundancy pay entirely.

2. Notice pay (if paid in lieu)

1 year or less1 week
More than 1 year, up to 3 years2 weeks
More than 3 years, up to 5 years3 weeks
More than 5 years4 weeks

Add an extra week if you're 45 or older with at least 2 years of service. Unlike redundancy pay, notice pay applies even at small businesses — there's no exemption. Your employer can either have you work the notice period, or pay you out instead ("payment in lieu").

3. Unused annual leave

Every employee is paid out any unused annual leave on termination, regardless of business size or reason for termination. It's simply your leave balance (in weeks) × your weekly pay — plus leave loading (commonly 17.5%) if your award or contract includes it.

Worked example

Scenario: an employee aged 47 has worked 5 years and 3 months at a company with 40 staff, earning $1,500/week, with 2 weeks of unused annual leave (no leave loading).

Does this apply to me if I'm a casual employee?

No. Casual employees are generally not covered by NES redundancy pay, notice, or annual leave provisions, regardless of how long they've worked for the employer.

What counts as "your pay" for this calculator?

Your ordinary rate of pay for your normal hours of work. It excludes overtime, bonuses, loadings, allowances, and superannuation contributions.

Is this payout taxed?

A genuine redundancy payment can be partly tax-free up to a limit set by the ATO each financial year, with amounts above that taxed as an employment termination payment (ETP). Notice pay and leave payouts are generally taxed differently again. This calculator estimates your NES entitlement only — speak to a tax professional or check the ATO website for how tax applies to your specific payout.

Do I get notice pay AND redundancy pay?

Yes, they're separate entitlements and both apply to a genuine redundancy. Notice pay compensates for the notice period (worked or paid in lieu); redundancy pay compensates for the job loss itself.

What if my employer has fewer than 15 employees?

Small business employers (fewer than 15 employees) are generally exempt from redundancy pay, but they still owe you notice pay and any unused annual leave in full — there's no small business exemption for those two. If you're an employer trying to manage redundancy obligations correctly, platforms like Employment Hero can help track entitlements and compliance.

What if I think my redundancy isn't genuine?

If you believe your role wasn't genuinely made redundant — for example, someone was hired to do the same job shortly after — you may have grounds for an unfair dismissal claim. You generally have 21 days from your termination date to lodge with the Fair Work Commission, so it's worth getting advice quickly. Services like LawPath connect you with employment lawyers for an initial consultation.

Can my award or contract give me more than this?

Yes. This calculator shows the NES minimums only. Many modern awards, enterprise agreements, and individual contracts provide higher entitlements — always check what applies to your role.
