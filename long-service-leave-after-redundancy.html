Long Service Leave Guide

Long service leave isn't part of the NES — here's what changes by state

Long service leave is one entitlement the calculator above deliberately leaves out — because unlike redundancy pay, it isn't set nationally.

Why LSL isn't in this calculator

Long service leave is one of the few employment entitlements not set by the National Employment Standards. Each state and territory has its own Long Service Leave Act, with different qualifying periods, accrual rates, and rules for what happens if your employment ends before you reach the standard milestone. That's why the redundancy calculator above deliberately leaves it out — it genuinely can't be estimated with a single national formula the way redundancy pay, notice, and annual leave can.

The general pattern across states

Most states set a standard qualifying period for long service leave — commonly somewhere around ten years of continuous service — but many also provide a shorter, pro-rata entitlement if your employment ends earlier for specific reasons, including redundancy, rather than requiring you to reach the full milestone. Exactly how many years you need for pro-rata access, and how much leave accrues per year, varies meaningfully between jurisdictions — a threshold that applies in one state won't necessarily apply in another, and some industry awards add their own portable long service leave schemes on top.

Why this matters at redundancy time

If you're anywhere near a long service leave milestone — or a pro-rata threshold — when you're made redundant, it's worth checking your state's specific Act before assuming you've missed out. Pro-rata redundancy provisions exist in most jurisdictions precisely to cover this situation, but the exact cut-off point and accrual calculation differ by state and sometimes by industry.

How it interacts with your redundancy payout

Where it's owed, long service leave is paid out separately from your NES redundancy pay, notice pay, and annual leave — it doesn't reduce or replace any of those entitlements, and it typically appears as its own line item on your final payslip rather than being folded into the total the calculator above estimates.

Where to check your state's rules

Because this varies by jurisdiction and can change, check your relevant state or territory long service leave authority for the exact qualifying period and pro-rata rules that apply to you — and check your award or enterprise agreement too, since some provide more generous terms than the statutory minimum.

Disclaimer: this guide explains the general concept and is not legal advice. Long service leave entitlements are set by state and territory legislation and vary by jurisdiction, industry, and individual circumstances — confirm your exact entitlement with your state's long service leave authority.
