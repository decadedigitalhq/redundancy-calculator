What's different in Victoria

For most Victorian employees, redundancy pay and notice are the same as the rest of Australia. Victoria has referred its workplace relations powers to the Commonwealth, so those come from the National Employment Standards (NES) in the Fair Work Act 2009. Some Victorian public sector employees have different arrangements, so check your enterprise agreement if that's you.

The real difference is long service leave. It isn't part of the NES. It comes from Victoria's Long Service Leave Act 2018, and on termination it can add several weeks' pay to your payout.

The formula is total weeks of continuous service ÷ 60. After at least 7 years with one employer, any unused long service leave is paid out when your employment ends, whatever the reason. Before 7 years, there's no payout under the Victorian Act.

  • Who it covers: full-time, part-time, casual, seasonal and fixed-term work, as long as the employment is continuous.
  • What doesn't break your service: up to 104 weeks of paid or unpaid parental leave, and absences of any length due to illness or injury.
  • Pay rate: normally your ordinary rate when the leave is paid. If your hours changed in recent years (say, from full-time to part-time), the Act uses an averaging rule, so the payout may differ from this estimate.
  • Who enforces it: Workforce Inspectorate Victoria (called Wage Inspectorate Victoria until December 2025).

Frequently asked questions

Is redundancy pay different in Victoria?

For most employees, no. Redundancy pay and notice follow the NES, the same as most of Australia. The scale runs from 4 weeks' pay at 1 year of service up to 16 weeks at 9 years, dropping to 12 weeks at 10 years or more. What's different in Victoria is long service leave.

Do I get long service leave paid out when I'm made redundant?

If you've had at least 7 years of continuous employment with the same employer, yes. Any unused long service leave is paid out when your employment ends. Under 7 years, there's no payout under the Victorian Act.

I'm a casual. Does any of this apply to me?

Long service leave, yes. Victoria's law covers casual and seasonal employees with continuous employment. NES redundancy pay, notice and annual leave generally don't apply to casuals, so for a casual the long service leave figure may be the main amount owed.

I'm a Victorian employer. Is this what I owe?

It's a minimum estimate. Enter the employee's service and ordinary pay, and tick the small business box if you have fewer than 15 employees. That exempts you from redundancy pay, but notice, annual leave and long service leave are still owed. Your award, enterprise agreement or contract may require more.

How is my payout taxed?

Each part is taxed differently. The tax-free part of a genuine redundancy is set by the ATO each year, and unused long service leave has its own rules. See our redundancy tax guide.
